Round seven

Five work page layouts that are not rows of cards. Different in kind, not in spacing.

Two things already done

The highlight is back to the block it was before any of this, on the site now. Three underlines were tried and none held: a plain bar read as half a rectangle, a tapered lens bulged at the ends, an even stroke read as flat. It was right the first time.

D1 is built on /services: outcome first, then who it is for, then what arrives as a ticked list, with a stage mark on each card. The deliverables in it are drafts I wrote from your service descriptions, marked as such in content/services.ts, and they need replacing with what you actually hand over.

The work page, five different kinds

Everything so far has been a row of cards in a different arrangement. These are not that.

X1

Colour fields

01 · Ice cream

Swirly

A playful, contemporary ice cream brand built to reimagine how indulgence looks and feels.

 02 · Clothing

Cultural symbolism and modern design, for a brand built on self-expression.

 03 · Hospitality

An identity drawn in sand, for a coastal retreat that never needed to shout.

 04 · Skincare

Lumeya

Conscious luxury skincare, built to refuse the visual language of its category.

The colours are sampled from your covers, not chosen by me. Swirly's cream, Asmi's teal, Paloma's sand, Lumeya's near-black. Scrolling the page becomes moving through each brand's own world, which is a thing only a brand designer's portfolio can honestly do. It is also the only layout here that would look wrong on anyone else's site.

Tall portrait crops in a row you push sideways, snapping to each. It feels like handling physical work rather than reading a page. I will say plainly that I would argue against it: people miss horizontal content, and a portfolio where the fifth project is invisible unless someone thinks to scroll is a portfolio with four projects.

Every image from every project, in square crops, grouped under the project name. It answers the question a work page is really being asked, which is how much have you actually done, and it uses the photography you already have rather than one hero shot per project. It is also the least designed of the five, deliberately: the work is the design.

One project per screen, full bleed, snapping as you scroll. The most dramatic and the most common: this is the layout every studio site has had for about eight years, so it will read as competent rather than as yours. It also puts a dark scrim over your covers, which is a strange thing to do to work chosen for its colour.

My reading, and it is not close: X1.

It is the only one of the twelve layouts I have shown you that could not be lifted onto another designer's site. The colours come from the work itself, so the page is literally made of your clients' brands, and scrolling it is the experience of moving between brand worlds. That is the argument your entire site makes, made into a layout.

X4 is the honest runner up and would suit you later, once there are fifteen projects and volume is the point rather than range.
