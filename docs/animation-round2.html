You were right, and here is the word for it

What you noticed is a real rule, not a preference.

Every animation on the site currently uses one easing curve: fast at the start, slowing into place. That is correct for anything that moves, because things that travel should arrive decisively and settle. It is wrong for anything that only fades, because opacity shoots almost to full in the first fraction of a second and then crawls. The thing appears, and then lingers. That is the abruptness you felt.

A fade wants to start slowly. Here are the four curves, then the same hero with each of them.

S5, and how it would work on your site

You asked exactly the right question. Here is the specific answer.

Every case study opens with a full width cover: /work/swirly, /work/paloma and so on. That image is already inside a fixed 3:2 frame and cropped to fill it, which means there is spare image on all four sides that nobody ever sees. S5 spends that spare.

The cover starts about ten percent larger than its frame and settles to true size over roughly a second as the page opens. Nothing is cut off that was not already being cut off, and no layout moves, because the frame itself never changes size. It is the cheapest possible motion: one transform on one element.

S5

A case study cover, at real proportions

scroll
SwirlyBrand identity and packaging
  • It replaces something. The covers already drift slightly as you scroll past, which you have seen. Doing both would be two motions on one image, so S5 would take that drift's place rather than join it.
  • It only fires once, on arrival. Not while scrolling. So it reads as the page opening rather than as a moving background.
  • Swirly and Paloma benefit most, because their sources are the ones with the most spare image outside the crop.
